Current Projects

Conditional Diffusion Model

Generative prediction of atmospheric temperature perturbations from WACCM-X.

Select the figure to view it full size.

This model learns the reverse diffusion process that turns zonal wind and a smoothed background temperature into a temperature perturbation field. Training uses 3-hourly WACCM-X samples at 16 fixed locations, packed into weekly input-target windows.

  • Separated background temperature from the perturbation field before constructing training pairs.
  • Conditioned generation on zonal wind and background temperature.
  • Checked held-out predictions with correlation, MAE, RMSE, relative total variation, and power spectra.

CAM-NET: Whole-Atmosphere Prediction

A spherical neural operator that emulates WACCM-X from the surface to the ionosphere.

CAM-NET is a geometry-aware Spherical Fourier Neural Operator trained on WACCM-X. A frozen atmospheric backbone predicts neutral winds, temperature, and vertical velocity; a lighter module then extends that state to electron density and zonal ion drift. On a held-out year it keeps the dominant ionosphere-thermosphere structure through multi-day rollouts, at about 1,000 times the speed of the physics model. It is meant for ensembles and sensitivity studies, not as a replacement for WACCM-X.

Ripple Recognition

Automatic detection of short-lived ripple structures in mesospheric airglow images.

Ripples are 5–15 km instability signatures near 87 km, and they are easy to miss by eye. A squeeze-and-excitation convolutional network, trained on 41×41 patches from all-sky OH images at Yucca Ridge, finds them with a consistent rule across a long image archive.

  • 92% F1 score on held-out image patches, with high precision and recall.
  • Recovers about 90% of a previous manual event catalog and adds faint events that the catalog missed.
  • Turns the detections into occurrence, season, and lifetime statistics.

Preprint on arXiv

CT-MVP: Data Imputation

A CNN-transformer that reconstructs long gaps in temperature and wind.

CT-MVP pairs convolutions, which capture local structure, with a transformer that follows dependencies across time and altitude. It was trained and tested on 13 years of SD-WACCM-X fields so that gap patterns could be controlled. It beats standard imputation methods when the missing stretches are long, and it is a step toward filling real gaps in lidar, radar, and satellite records.
